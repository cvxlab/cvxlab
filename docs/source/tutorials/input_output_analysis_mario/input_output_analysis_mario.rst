.. _tutorial-ioa-mario:

Economy-wide impact assessment
==============================

This tutorial is designed as a **twin tutorial** with the
`MARIO <https://www.marioproject.com/en/latest/index.html>`_ Python package.
The two parts address the same economy-wide impact assessment using Leontief's
Input-Output Analysis, covering complementary stages of the workflow:

- **The MARIO tutorial**, hosted in the MARIO documentation, explains how to
  obtain the source Supply and Use Table (SUT), process the dataset, and prepare
  the data to be supplied as inputs to the CVXlab model.
- **The CVXlab tutorial (this page)** explains how to organize the prepared data
  into model data structures, define and generate the mathematical model, solve
  it for the selected scenarios, and export and interpret the results.

Follow the MARIO part to prepare the reference dataset, then use this page to
build and solve the model. The prepared SUT is the link between the two parts.

Knowledge of Input-Output Analysis is required to follow this tutorial. The MARIO 
documentation provides an introductory overview of the theory and its applications.

Before starting, set up a Conda environment with both CVXlab and MARIO packages 
installed. See :doc:`CVXlab installation </installation>` and
`MARIO getting started <https://www.marioproject.com/en/latest/get_started/index.html>`_
for setup instructions.


.. _ioa_mario_problem_statement:

.. admonition:: Problem statement

  How would changes in Italy's electricity supply affect **value added**,
  **imports**, and **GHG emissions** across the economy?

  Use an input-output model to compare the **Reference** system with two hypothetical
  electricity-supply scenarios:

  - **High_RES** change the domestic electricity generation mix to the High RES
    shares below.
  - **High_RES_domestic** apply the same High RES generation mix
    and replace all electricity imports with domestic generation. Intermediate
    and final demand for imported electricity are redirected to domestic
    electricity, so electricity imports are zero.

  The two scenarios have the same domestic generation mix and differ only in
  their electricity import assumptions. The shares below are illustrative
  assumptions for this teaching exercise, not observed data or policy targets.

  .. math::

    \begin{array}{lrr}
    \text{Electricity generation by...}
      & \text{Reference (y2022)}
      & \text{High RES} \\
    \hline
    \text{Coal}               &  9.7\% & - \\
    \text{Gas}                & 15.8\% & - \\
    \text{Nuclear}            & 23.4\% & 15.0\% \\
    \text{Hydro}              & 13.3\% &  9.0\% \\
    \text{Wind}               & 17.5\% & 38.0\% \\
    \text{Petroleum}          &  1.6\% & - \\
    \text{Biomass and Waste}  &  6.4\% &  5.0\% \\
    \text{Solar photovoltaic} & 11.0\% & 31.0\% \\
    \text{Tide, Wave, Ocean}  & -       &  0.5\% \\
    \text{Geothermal}         &  0.2\% &  1.5\% \\
    \text{Others}             &  1.1\% & - \\
    \end{array}

  Calculate the changes in *value added*, *imports*, and *GHG emissions* for each
  scenario relative to Reference. Then compare the two High RES scenarios to
  isolate the additional effect of replacing electricity imports with domestic
  generation.

  In High RES + domestic substitution, only electricity imports are eliminated.
  Imports of fuels and other goods used by domestic producers remain possible.
  Assume that all changes occur overnight (comparative statics analysis).

  This is a teaching exercise exploring the economy-wide implications of
  assumed structural changes. It does not assess the technical feasibility
  of the electricity mix or provide a forecast of Italy's energy transition.

.. _ioa_mario_modelling_approach:

Modelling approach
------------------

Before translating the model into CVXlab Sets, Data Tables, Variables, and
Problems, this section introduces the mathematical relationships used to assess
the scenario. The workflow is:

1. Start from a reference Supply and Use Table (SUT).
2. Derive the coefficients needed to describe the reference system.
3. Apply the electricity-supply scenario assumptions.
4. Solve the quantity model and reconstruct the scenario SUT.
5. Compare value added, imports, and GHG emissions with the reference system.


.. rubric:: Reference system

The starting point is Italy's national Supply and Use Table (SUT), extracted
from the monetary version of the **EXIOBASE multi-regional input-output (MRIO)**
database for the reference year 2022. All monetary transactions are expressed in
million euros (M€), while greenhouse-gas (GHG) emissions are expressed in
kilotonnes (kton). The SUT has the following structure:

.. math::

  \begin{array}{l|cccc}
  & \begin{array}{c}\text{Products}\\\text{(domestic)}\end{array} 
    & \text{Technologies} 
    & \begin{array}{c}\text{Final}\\\text{Demand}\end{array}
    & \text{Total} \\
  \hline
  \text{Products (domestic)} & - & \mathbf{U}_0 & \mathbf{Y}_0 & \mathbf{Q}_0 \\
  \text{Technologies} & \mathbf{V}_0 & - & - & \mathbf{X}_0 \\
  \text{Products (imports)} & - & \mathbf{M}_{u,0} & \mathbf{M}_{y,0} & - \\
  \text{Value added} & - & \mathbf{F}_{u,0} & \mathbf{F}_{y,0} & - \\
  \text{Environmental flows} & - & \mathbf{R}_{u,0} & \mathbf{R}_{y,0} & - \\
  \end{array}

The subscript :math:`0` denotes the reference accounting period. Each symbol 
represents a block of entries defined as follows:

- Make table (:math:`\mathbf{V}_0`): products supplied by each domestic technology.
- Use table (:math:`\mathbf{U}_0`): products used by each technology.
- Final demand table (:math:`\mathbf{Y}_0`): products final demand.
- Intermediate and final imports (:math:`\mathbf{M}_{u,0}` and :math:`\mathbf{M}_{y,0}`): 
  products imported for intermediate and final use.
- Value added table (:math:`\mathbf{F}_{u,0}` and :math:`\mathbf{F}_{y,0}`): value 
  added by each technology.
- Environmental flows table (:math:`\mathbf{R}_{u,0}` and :math:`\mathbf{R}_{y,0}`): 
  environmental impacts associated with each technology and final demand category.

- :math:`\mathbf{Q}_0`: exogenous reference domestic product output vector.
- :math:`\mathbf{X}_0`: exogenous reference technology activity vector.

Assuming that the SUT above is available, the model can be defined as **two sequential 
numerical problems** described below.


.. rubric:: Problem 1 - Data Processing (DP)

The exogenous scenario shocks follow the same block layout as the reference
SUT. Changes to intermediate use, supply, and intermediate imports are specified
as coefficient changes; changes to final demand and final imports are specified
as monetary flows. Dashes indicate blocks without user-defined shocks in this
formulation.

.. math::

  \begin{array}{l|ccc}
  & \begin{array}{c}\text{Products}\\\text{(domestic)}\end{array}
    & \text{Technologies}
    & \begin{array}{c}\text{Final}\\\text{Demand}\end{array} \\
  \hline
  \text{Products (domestic)} & - & \Delta\mathbf{u} & \Delta\mathbf{Y} \\
  \text{Technologies} & \Delta\mathbf{d} & - & - \\
  \text{Products (imports)} & - & \Delta\mathbf{m}_u & \Delta\mathbf{M}_y \\
  \end{array}

- :math:`\Delta\mathbf{u}`: additive scenario changes to domestic input coefficients.
- :math:`\Delta\mathbf{d}`: additive scenario changes to technology market shares.
- :math:`\Delta\mathbf{m}_u`: additive scenario changes to import coefficients.
- :math:`\Delta\mathbf{Y}`: additive scenario change to domestic final demand.
- :math:`\Delta\mathbf{M}_y`: additive scenario change to final imports.

This problem calculates the coefficient tables from the reference SUT, applying
user-defined changes to the coefficients. Reference product output and technology
activity are supplied as exogenous totals in the reference SUT.

The following equations define the scenario coefficients (notice that the operator 
:math:`\widehat{\mathbf{v}}` defines diagonal matrix whose diagonal contains the 
entries of vector :math:`\mathbf{v}`). 

.. math::

  \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{U}_0\,\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1}
    + \Delta\mathbf{u} \\ 
  \mathbf{d} = \mathbf{V}_0\,\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_0^{-1}
    + \Delta\mathbf{d} \\
  \mathbf{m}_u = \mathbf{M}_{u,0}\,\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1}
    + \Delta\mathbf{m}_u \\
  \mathbf{f} = \mathbf{F}_{u,0}\,\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1} \\
  \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{R}_{u,0}\,\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1}


Endogenous variables are defined as follows: 

- :math:`\mathbf{u}`: domestic product input coefficients per unit of technology activity.
- :math:`\mathbf{d}`: technology market shares in domestic product supply.
- :math:`\mathbf{m}_u`: imported product input coefficients per unit of technology activity.
- :math:`\mathbf{f}`: value added coefficients per unit of technology activity.
- :math:`\mathbf{r}`: environmental flow coefficients per unit of technology activity.


.. rubric:: Problem 2 - Impact analysis (IA)

The actual Leontief quantity model consists of the following two simultaneous
equations. They determine total products demand and total technology activity using
the coefficient tables obtained from data processing as exogenous inputs. Domestic
final demand :math:`\mathbf{Y}` is prescribed by the scenario, as defined in the
table below.

.. math::

  \begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{Q} &= \mathbf{u}\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y}\mathbf{i}_y \\
  \mathbf{X} &= \mathbf{d}\mathbf{Q}
  \end{aligned}

- :math:`\mathbf{Q}`: endogenous domestic product output vector.
- :math:`\mathbf{X}`: endogenous technology activity vector.
- :math:`\mathbf{i}_y`: column vector of ones with one entry per final-demand
  category; it reduces to the scalar one when final demand has a single column.

The table combines the prescribed scenario inputs with the derived endogenous
flows that reconstruct the scenario SUT. Final demand and final imports follow
the user-defined shocks; the intermediate flows and make table are derived from
the solved :math:`\mathbf{Q}` and :math:`\mathbf{X}`. Value added and environmental
flows associated with final demand retain their reference values.

.. math::

  \begin{array}{l|ccc}
  & \begin{array}{c}\text{Products}\\\text{(domestic)}\end{array}
    & \text{Technologies}
    & \begin{array}{c}\text{Final}\\\text{Demand}\end{array} \\
  \hline
  \text{Products (domestic)} & - & \mathbf{U}=\mathbf{u}\widehat{\mathbf{X}} & \mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{Y}_0+\Delta\mathbf{Y} \\
  \text{Technologies} & \mathbf{V}=\mathbf{d}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}} & - & - \\
  \text{Products (imports)} & - & \mathbf{M}_u=\mathbf{m}_u\widehat{\mathbf{X}} & \mathbf{M}_y=\mathbf{M}_{y,0}+\Delta\mathbf{M}_y \\
  \text{Value added} & - & \mathbf{F}_u=\mathbf{f}\widehat{\mathbf{X}} & \mathbf{F}_y=\mathbf{F}_{y,0} \\
  \text{Environmental flows} & - & \mathbf{R}_u=\mathbf{r}\widehat{\mathbf{X}} & \mathbf{R}_y=\mathbf{R}_{y,0} \\
  \end{array}

- :math:`\mathbf{Y}`: prescribed scenario domestic final demand.
- :math:`\mathbf{M}_y`: prescribed scenario final imports.
- :math:`\mathbf{U}`: derived endogenous use table.
- :math:`\mathbf{V}`: derived endogenous make table.
- :math:`\mathbf{M}_u`: derived endogenous intermediate imports table.
- :math:`\mathbf{F}_u`: derived endogenous value added by technology.
- :math:`\mathbf{R}_u`: derived endogenous environmental flows by technology.
- :math:`\mathbf{F}_y` and :math:`\mathbf{R}_y`: value added and environmental
  flows associated with final demand, held at their reference values.

The impact of the analyzed structural changes is assessed by comparing the
reference SUT with the reconstructed scenario SUT.

Conceptual model definition
---------------------------

Related user guide step: :ref:`conceptual-model-definition`.

In this step, the mathematical formulation introduced in :ref:`ioa_mario_modelling_approach` 
is represented as CVXlab Sets, Data Tables, Variables, and Problems abstractions. 
The prepared SUT provides the reference data and the classifications used for the 
model coordinates.

The definitions below connect the mathematical quantities to their CVXlab
abstractions and specify the dependency between data processing and impact
analysis.


.. rubric:: Defining Sets

The model uses four Sets, defined in ``structure_sets.yml``. Products, accounts,
and extensions define the accounting domains of the fully disaggregated Italian
SUT, while scenarios identify the cases to be simulated.

.. list-table:: Sets of the tutorial model
  :header-rows: 1
  :widths: 30 15 25 30

  * - Set name
    - Symbol
    - Cardinality
    - Set type
  * - products
    - :math:`p`
    - 189
    - Dimension set
  * - accounts
    - :math:`a`
    - 172
    - Dimension set
  * - extensions
    - :math:`e`
    - 13
    - Dimension set
  * - scenarios
    - :math:`s`
    - 3
    - Inter-problem set

Notice that:

- The complete coordinates and filter assignments are provided in ``sets.xlsx``
  in the tutorial model materials. The cardinalities of the sets refer to the 
  classifications retained from the Italian SUT: 189 products, 163 production
  technologies, 9 final-demand categories, 12 value-added categories, and one
  aggregated greenhouse-gas emissions category.
- ``accounts`` combines production technologies and final-demand categories in
  one accounting domain of 172 coordinates. Its ``category`` filter distinguishes
  163 ``intermediate`` accounts from 9 ``final`` accounts. Intermediate accounts represent technologies as both
  suppliers and users of products; final accounts represent final-demand
  categories. These subsets select the appropriate rows or columns of the model
  variables, allowing intermediate and final flows to share a Data Table.
- The ``type: power`` filter identifies electricity-related products in
  ``products`` and electricity-generating technologies in ``accounts``. These
  selections identify the products and technologies affected by the
  electricity-supply shocks.
- ``extensions`` groups value added and environmental flows. Its ``category``
  filter distinguishes 12 ``value_added`` coordinates from one
  ``environmental_flows`` coordinate, for a total of 13 extensions. This allows
  the two kinds of extensions to share a Data Table while defining separate
  variables for their flows and coefficients.
- ``scenarios`` is the only Set with ``split_problem: true``. It generates three
  instances of each symbolic problem, one for each scenario. 
- The reference SUT data are shared across scenarios. Scenario-dependent shocks
  distinguish ``High_RES`` from ``Reference`` and add the substitution of imported
  electricity in ``High_RES_domestic``.


.. rubric:: Defining Data Tables and related Variables

The Data Tables and variables below follow ``structure_variables.yml``. The table
domains use the Set symbols introduced above. DP and IA denote the
``data_processing`` and ``impact_analysis`` problems, respectively.

.. dropdown:: Show the Data Tables of the tutorial model

   .. include:: data_tables.rst.inc

Notice that:

- Reference Data Tables do not include ``scenarios`` in their domains, so the same
  reference values are used for all scenarios. Shock tables include ``scenarios``
  and can therefore contain different changes for each case.
- The four coefficient Data Tables have problem-dependent types: their variables
  are endogenous in DP and become exogenous inputs to IA. The remaining tables
  marked endogenous contain the output, activity, and accounting flows used in
  impact analysis.
- Domain cardinalities count all combinations of the source table coordinates.
  For example, ``use`` has :math:`189 \times 172 \times 3 = 97\,524` rows,
  including all three scenarios. Filters select the relevant
  subsets for individual variables without changing the source table domain.
- ``sum_vector_y`` is a constant Data Table. Its variable ``i_y`` uses the built-in
  ``value: sum_vector`` definition and selects only final-demand accounts.

Variable shapes refer to the following filtered subsets:

- :math:`a_i` and :math:`a_f`: ``accounts`` selected with
  ``category: intermediate`` and ``category: final``, with
  :math:`|a_i|=163` and :math:`|a_f|=9`, respectively.
- :math:`e_v` and :math:`e_r`: ``extensions`` selected with
  ``category: value_added`` and ``category: environmental_flows``, with
  :math:`|e_v|=12` and :math:`|e_r|=1`, respectively.

The variable names below are the exact keys used in the setup file and symbolic
expressions. Shapes are reported as numbers of rows times numbers of columns.

.. dropdown:: Show the Variables of the tutorial model

   .. include:: variables.rst.inc

Regarding the variables above:

- A variable inherits its type and domain from its source Data Table. Assigning
  Sets to rows and columns determines its matrix shape; an unassigned matrix
  dimension has size one. In particular, ``Q``, ``X``, and ``i_y`` are column
  vectors.
- The same Data Table can define multiple variables through filters. For example,
  ``use`` provides ``U`` on intermediate accounts and ``Y`` on final accounts.
  ``extensions`` combines the account and extension filters to define ``F_u``,
  ``F_y``, ``R_u``, and ``R_y``.
- Supply variables ``V_0``, ``delta_d``, ``d``, and ``V`` have intermediate accounts
  as rows and products as columns. Use and import variables have products as
  rows and the selected account categories as columns.
- No variable has intra-problem sets: all dimension Sets are assigned to matrix
  rows or columns. Variables indexed by ``scenarios`` have three instances, one
  per scenario; reference variables and the constant ``i_y`` have no scenario
  index.
- The setup specifies ``blank_fill: 0`` for the shock and coefficient variables.
  Unfilled entries in those variables are treated as zeros. The constant
  ``i_y`` is generated from its definition rather than supplied as numerical
  input data.
  

.. rubric:: Defining Problem and related Expressions

The setup file ``problem.yml`` defines two sequential symbolic problems:
``data_processing`` and ``impact_analysis``. Both are systems of linear equalities
with no user-defined objective function; their purpose is to find values that
satisfy the specified relationships.

**Problem 1: data_processing**

For each scenario, this problem calculates the five coefficient tables from the
reference SUT and the prescribed coefficient shocks:

.. math::

  \begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{u} &= \mathbf{U}_0\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1} + \Delta\mathbf{u} \\
  \mathbf{d} &= \mathbf{V}_0\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_0^{-1} + \Delta\mathbf{d} \\
  \mathbf{m}_u &= \mathbf{M}_{u,0}\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1} + \Delta\mathbf{m}_u \\
  \mathbf{f} &= \mathbf{F}_{u,0}\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1} \\
  \mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{R}_{u,0}\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_0^{-1}
  \end{aligned}

The resulting variables ``u``, ``d``, ``m_u``, ``f``, and ``r`` are endogenous in
this problem. Their solved values become exogenous inputs to ``impact_analysis``
through the problem-dependent types defined in ``structure_variables.yml``.

**Problem 2: impact_analysis**

For each scenario, this problem determines product output and technology activity,
applies the final-demand and final-import shocks, and reconstructs the SUT flows:

.. math::

  \begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{Q} &= \mathbf{u}\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y}\mathbf{i}_y \\
  \mathbf{X} &= \mathbf{d}\mathbf{Q} \\
  \mathbf{Y} &= \mathbf{Y}_0 + \Delta\mathbf{Y} \\
  \mathbf{U} &= \mathbf{u}\widehat{\mathbf{X}} \\
  \mathbf{V} &= \mathbf{d}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}} \\
  \mathbf{M}_u &= \mathbf{m}_u\widehat{\mathbf{X}} \\
  \mathbf{M}_y &= \mathbf{M}_{y,0} + \Delta\mathbf{M}_y \\
  \mathbf{F}_u &= \mathbf{f}\widehat{\mathbf{X}} \\
  \mathbf{F}_y &= \mathbf{F}_{y,0} \\
  \mathbf{R}_u &= \mathbf{r}\widehat{\mathbf{X}} \\
  \mathbf{R}_y &= \mathbf{R}_{y,0}
  \end{aligned}

The first two equations are the Leontief quantity model. The remaining equations
define scenario final demand and imports and the derived accounting flows.
In particular, ``Y`` and ``M_y`` are calculated in ``impact_analysis``, as specified
in ``problem.yml``; their values are prescribed by reference data and shocks.

Notice that:

- ``scenarios`` generates three instances of each symbolic problem, corresponding
  to ``Reference``, ``High_RES``, and ``High_RES_domestic``. For each scenario,
  ``data_processing`` must be solved before ``impact_analysis`` so that the latter
  receives the corresponding coefficient values.
- No variable is indexed over intra-problem sets. Each symbolic expression
  therefore defines one matrix or vector equality per scenario, representing
  elementwise equalities over its rows and columns.
- In the setup expressions, ``==`` denotes equality and ``@`` denotes matrix
  multiplication. ``diag(x)`` constructs the diagonal matrix represented here
  by a hat, and ``minv(diag(x))`` represents its inverse.
- ``i_y`` is the constant column vector of ones on final-demand accounts, so
  ``Y @ i_y`` sums domestic final demand across its categories.

The following implementation steps assume that the reference SUT has been
prepared following the companion MARIO tutorial. Its classifications define the
model coordinates, while its numerical entries populate the reference Data Tables.

Generation of model directory
-----------------------------

| Related user guide step: :ref:`generation-of-model-directory`
| Related API: :py:func:`cvxlab.create_model_dir`

This step consists of creating the model directory scaffold with blank settings 
file(s) that will later host, sets file, input data and database.

.. code-block:: python

    import cvxlab

    cvxlab.create_model_dir(
        settings_file_type='yml', # use 'xlsx' for model_settings.xlsx
    )

Notice that:

- If arguments ``model_dir_name`` and ``main_dir_path`` are not specified, the 
  model directory is created with default name *model* in the *current working 
  directory*.
- The argument ``settings_file_type`` defines the format of the setup files. Both
  YAML and Excel formats are supported. The tutorial materials include setup files 
  in both formats, so the tutorial can be followed regardless of the choice made 
  at this stage.
- At this stage, only the model directory and the setup file templates are 
  generated. The sets file, input data file(s), and SQLite database are created 
  in later steps.

  - For ``settings_file_type='yml'``: three YAML files are generated in the 
    *model* directory, named ``structure_sets.yml``, ``structure_variables.yml``, 
    and ``problem.yml``.
  - For ``settings_file_type='xlsx'``: a single Excel workbook named 
    ``model_settings.xlsx`` is generated in the *model* directory, with three tabs 
    named ``structure_sets``, ``structure_variables``, and ``problem``.

Model class instance generation
-------------------------------

| Related user guide step: :ref:`generate-model-class-instance`
| Related class constructor: :py:class:`cvxlab.Model`

Create a ``Model`` instance after saving the setup files. Run the following code
from the directory containing ``model`` and retain the same Python session for
the subsequent steps.

.. code-block:: python

  import cvxlab

  model = cvxlab.Model(
      log_level='debug',
      model_settings_from='yml',  # use 'xlsx' for model_settings.xlsx
      use_existing_data=False,
      multiple_input_files=False,
      input_data_files_type='xlsx',
      detailed_validation=True,
  )

With ``use_existing_data=False``, CVXlab parses the setup files and generates a
blank ``sets.xlsx`` workbook. Fill it before initializing the model environment.
The SQLite database and input-data workbook are generated later.

Notice that:

- The default model directory is ``model`` in the current working directory.
- ``multiple_input_files=False`` collects the generated input tables in a single
  Excel workbook, with one worksheet per input Data Table.
- ``detailed_validation=True`` provides more detailed validation messages while
  assembling the model.
- ``use_existing_data=True`` is intended for a model whose coordinates, database,
  and input files already exist. The from-scratch workflow below uses ``False``.


Fill sets data (model coordinates)
----------------------------------

Related user guide step: :ref:`fill-sets-data`

Fill the generated ``model/sets.xlsx`` using the classifications of the dataset
prepared in the companion MARIO tutorial. Products, technologies, final-demand
categories, and extensions must match the prepared reference SUT, including its
level of disaggregation. These classifications define the coordinates used to
align the numerical inputs in the subsequent data-filling step.

The complete coordinate and filter assignments are provided in the tutorial model
materials: :download:`sets.xlsx <materials/model/sets.xlsx>`.

The workbook has four worksheets. Keep the generated worksheet names and column
headers unchanged, and fill the coordinate, filter, and aggregation columns as
summarized below.

   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 20 60

   * - Worksheet
     - Columns
     - Content
   * - _set_PRODUCTS
     - products_Name, products_type, products_agg_medium,
       products_agg_high
     - Product coordinates; power identifies electricity products.
       The aggregation columns map products to the corresponding groups.
   * - _set_ACCOUNTS
     - accounts_Name, accounts_category, accounts_type,
       accounts_agg_medium, accounts_agg_high
     - Technology and final-demand coordinates. Assign intermediate or
       final to accounts_category and power to electricity-generating
       technologies. Fill the aggregation mappings consistently with the materials.
   * - _set_EXTENSIONS
     - extensions_Name, extensions_category
     - Value added and environmental-flow coordinates, classified as
       value_added or environmental_flows.
   * - _set_SCENARIOS
     - scenarios_Name
     - Reference, High_RES, High_RES_domestic.

Save the workbook before continuing. Filters determine which coordinates appear
in each variable, so their assignments must agree with the intended SUT blocks.


Initialization of data structures
---------------------------------

| Related user guide step: :ref:`data-structures-init`
| Related API: :py:meth:`~cvxlab.Model.initialize_model_environment`

After filling and saving ``sets.xlsx``, initialize the model environment:

.. code-block:: python

  model.initialize_model_environment()

CVXlab loads the coordinates and generates ``model/database.db`` and
``model/input_data/input_data.xlsx``. Their structure follows the setup files and
the supplied coordinates. The database set tables are populated, while the
numerical input worksheets are ready to be filled in the next step.


.. _ioa_mario_fill_exogenous_data:

Fill exogenous model data
-------------------------

Related user guide step: :ref:`fill-exogenous-data`

Use the reference SUT obtained by following the companion MARIO tutorial to
populate the reference Data Tables in ``model/input_data/input_data.xlsx``.
The table below maps the prepared SUT blocks to the CVXlab input worksheets and
identifies the additional scenario shocks defined in
:ref:`ioa_mario_modelling_approach`.

Fill the ``values`` column by matching the generated coordinate columns to the
prepared data, using the same classifications entered in ``sets.xlsx``. Keep the
``id`` and coordinate columns unchanged; their ordering need not match the row
or column ordering of the original matrices.

.. list-table:: 
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 25 55

   * - Worksheet
     - Variable
     - Values to enter
   * - make_0
     - :math:`\mathbf{V}_0`
     - Reference supply flows on intermediate accounts.
   * - use_0
     - :math:`\mathbf{U}_0`, :math:`\mathbf{Y}_0`
     - Reference domestic intermediate use and final demand.
   * - imports_0
     - :math:`\mathbf{M}_{u,0}`, :math:`\mathbf{M}_{y,0}`
     - Reference intermediate and final imports.
   * - extensions_0
     - :math:`\mathbf{F}_{u,0}`, :math:`\mathbf{F}_{y,0}`,
       :math:`\mathbf{R}_{u,0}`, :math:`\mathbf{R}_{y,0}`
     - Reference value added and environmental flows for the selected accounts.
   * - total_demand_0
     - :math:`\mathbf{Q}_0`
     - Reference domestic product output totals.
   * - total_activity_0
     - :math:`\mathbf{X}_0`
     - Reference activity totals on intermediate accounts.
   * - delta_make
     - :math:`\Delta\mathbf{d}`
     - Additive changes to technology market shares, by scenario.
   * - delta_use
     - :math:`\Delta\mathbf{u}`, :math:`\Delta\mathbf{Y}`
     - Additive changes to domestic use coefficients and final demand, by scenario.
   * - delta_imports
     - :math:`\Delta\mathbf{m}_u`, :math:`\Delta\mathbf{M}_y`
     - Additive changes to import coefficients and final imports, by scenario.

Reference values are shared across scenarios. Enter zero shocks for ``Reference``.
For ``High_RES``, change the electricity technology market shares while keeping
electricity import assumptions unchanged. For ``High_RES_domestic``, apply the
same generation mix and also transfer imported electricity to domestic supply:
reduce electricity import coefficients and final imports, and increase the
corresponding domestic use coefficients and final demand.

The generated workbook also contains coefficient worksheets because those tables
are exogenous in ``impact_analysis``. Their values are calculated by
``data_processing`` and transferred during sequential solution; they are not
additional reference-data inputs to fill manually. The constant ``i_y`` is
generated from its ``sum_vector`` definition.

The supplied ``model/input_data/input_data.xlsx`` file provides the model's numerical 
inputs. Its coordinate domain must match the ``sets.xlsx`` used to generate the 
working workbook. Save the filled input workbook before initializing the numerical 
problems.


Initialization of numerical problem(s)
--------------------------------------

| Related user guide step: :ref:`numerical-problem-init`
| Related API: :py:meth:`~cvxlab.Model.refresh_database_and_initialize_problem`

Import the input data into SQLite and initialize the numerical problems:

.. code-block:: python

  model.refresh_database_and_initialize_problem()

CVXlab validates the symbolic expressions and creates the CVXPY variables and
problems. Each of the two symbolic problems has three scenario instances: one
for ``Reference``, one for ``High_RES``, and one for ``High_RES_domestic``.
Coefficient values used by the second problem are supplied by the first during
sequential solution.


Solution of numerical problem(s)
--------------------------------

| Related user guide step: :ref:`numerical-problem-run`
| Related API: :py:meth:`~cvxlab.Model.run_model`

Solve the problems sequentially, following the chain used in the supplied
``run.py``:

.. code-block:: python

  model.run_model(
      solution_mode='sequential',
      sequential_solution_chain=['data_processing', 'impact_analysis'],
  )

The ``data_processing`` results populate the coefficient parameters used by
``impact_analysis`` for the corresponding scenario. The five coefficient
variables therefore change role from endogenous in the first problem to
exogenous in the second, as specified in their Data Table definitions.

Argument ``solver`` can be set to a specific installed solver if compatible CVXPY. 
Inspect the logged status of each problem instance before using its results. 
After successful solution, endogenous values are available in memory; the next 
step exports them to SQLite.


Export endogenous model data
----------------------------

| Related user guide step: :ref:`export-model-results`
| Related API: :py:meth:`~cvxlab.Model.load_results_to_database`

Export the solved endogenous values to the model database:

.. code-block:: python

  model.load_results_to_database()

The coefficient and impact-analysis tables in ``model/database.db`` now provide
the solved scenario values for inspection and comparison.


.. _ioa_mario_compare_results:

Compare and interpret the results
---------------------------------

There are several ways to inspect and visualize the results stored in
``model/database.db``. For convenience, this tutorial uses Power BI: the linked
``report.pbix`` file is provided in the tutorial materials and supports interactive
exploration of the reference data and scenario results.

Power BI is optional. The same information can be accessed directly through
SQLite queries or a database browser, or loaded into Python using ``sqlite3``
and ``pandas``. Figures can then be created with libraries such as Matplotlib
or Plotly, according to the user's preferred analysis workflow.

Use the report to explore the effects of the electricity-supply changes and
compare ``High_RES`` and ``High_RES_domestic`` with ``Reference``. Comparing the
two High RES scenarios highlights the additional effect of replacing electricity
imports with domestic generation.
